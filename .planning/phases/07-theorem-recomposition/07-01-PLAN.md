**Step 1: faulty -> RTL3** (backward simulation from RTLtolerant)
- Apply check_program_sound to get wc_program tp from the check_program hypothesis
- Apply faulty_backward_simulation (from RTLtolerant) to get backward_simulation (RTL3.semantics tp) (faulty_semantics tp)

**Step 2: RTL3 -> RTL** (Phase 5 bridge from RTLagreement)
- Apply rtl_weak_agreement_no_novotes tp to beh3 to get: beh2 in RTL(tp) with behavior_improves beh3 beh2

**Step 3: RTL -> C** (standard backward simulation from Compiler.v)
- Apply transf_c_program_to_rtl_correct to get backward_simulation (Csem.semantics p) (RTL.semantics tp)
- Apply backward_simulation_behavior_improves to beh2 to get: beh_c in Csem(p) with behavior_improves beh_c beh2

**Diamond resolution** (compose behavior_improves beh_c beh2 + beh3 beh2 + beh3 beh -> beh_c beh):
The three behavior_improves relations form a diamond (beh3 points to both beh2 and beh,
beh_c points to beh2). Resolve by case analysis on behavior_improves beh3 beh2:

- Case beh3 = beh2: Chain behavior_improves beh_c beh3 + behavior_improves beh3 beh
  via behavior_improves_trans. Done.

- Case beh3 = Goes_wrong t with behavior_prefix t beh2: Sub-case analysis on
  behavior_improves beh3 beh:

  - Sub-case beh = Goes_wrong t (beh3 = beh): Then behavior_improves beh_c beh2 and
    beh2 extends past t. Sub-case on behavior_improves beh_c beh2:
    - beh_c = beh2: right. exists t; split; auto.
    - beh_c = Goes_wrong t'' with behavior_prefix t'' beh2: Both t'' and t are
      trace-prefixes of the same behavior beh2. Prove a helper lemma that two
      behavior_prefix of the same behavior are comparable (one is a prefix of the
      other as traces). If t'' prefix of t: behavior_improves (Goes_wrong t'')
      (Goes_wrong t) holds. If t prefix of t'': then t'' extends past t into beh2
      territory.

      IMPORTANT: The case "t prefix of t''" (C goes wrong LATER than RTL3) requires
      careful handling. Two sub-sub-cases:
      (a) If t'' is prefix of beh (the faulty behavior): behavior_improves (Goes_wrong t'') beh. Done.
      (b) If t'' is NOT prefix of beh: this means the C program and faulty program
          diverged after trace t. In this scenario, one must show that the C program
          cannot go wrong at a point that is reachable only through the RTL (non-faulty)
          execution path but NOT through the faulty execution path.

          The key argument: behavior_improves beh_c beh2 says beh_c is at most as good
          as beh2. beh_c = Goes_wrong t''. behavior_improves (Goes_wrong t) beh2 says
          t is a prefix of beh2. behavior_improves (Goes_wrong t) beh says t is a prefix
          of beh. So beh and beh2 share prefix t but may diverge after t. t'' extends
          past t along beh2's path. If beh2 and beh share MORE than just t (they might
          share a longer common prefix), then t'' might still be a prefix of beh.

          PRACTICAL RESOLUTION: If this case proves difficult, use Admitted on JUST this
          edge case and document as a follow-up. However, try the following argument
          first: since behavior_improves beh_c beh2 and behavior_improves beh3 beh and
          beh3 = Goes_wrong t, we can deduce behavior_improves beh_c beh by showing:
          if beh_c = Goes_wrong t'', then t'' must be a prefix of beh because t'' is a
          prefix of beh2's trace, and beh and beh2 share the same trace up to at least t.
          Since t is a prefix of t'', and t is a prefix of beh, if t'' extends past t,
          we need the faulty trace after t to match beh2's trace up to t''. This follows
          from: the faulty execution takes the same steps as RTL up to the fault point,
          and the fault occurs at or after the first vote instruction AFTER t. Before the
          fault, faulty and RTL agree. So the faulty trace after t matches RTL's trace
          for some prefix, and t'' (being a prefix of the RTL trace) might fall within
          this agreement zone.

          If ALL ELSE FAILS with the edge case: consider whether the edge case actually
          arises for well-colored programs. In non-faulty RTL3 execution of a well-colored
          TMR'd program, vote3(a,a,a) = a (identical well-typed replicas). So RTL3 should
          never go wrong at vote instructions. If beh3 = Goes_wrong t, then it must be a
          non-vote failure, in which case RTL ALSO goes wrong at t (identical step for
          non-vote instructions), so beh2 = Goes_wrong t = beh3. This eliminates the
          diamond entirely. To formalize: prove that for non-vote instructions,
          RTL3.step and RTL.step are identical, so if RTL3 is stuck at a non-vote
          instruction, RTL is also stuck.
