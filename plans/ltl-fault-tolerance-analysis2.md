**Findings**
1. The biggest conceptual mismatch is the fault model around builtins. The plan's `V1` explicitly includes "protected non-vote, non-external builtins" at [plans/ltl-fault-tolerance.md#L148](/home/alex/source/compcert/plans/ltl-fault-tolerance.md#L148), but the current RTL faulty model forbids zapping builtins entirely at [backend/RTLfault.v#L52](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/RTLfault.v#L52). Also, in LTL every `Lbuiltin` steps via `external_call` at [backend/LTL.v#L246](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/LTL.v#L246), so "non-external builtin" is not really an LTL semantic category. If you keep builtin-result faults in `V1`, you are proving a materially stronger and less RTL-analogous theorem.

2. The plan underestimates how much of the needed post-allocation structure already lives inside `Allocation`/`Allocproof`. `block_shape` already classifies the RTL-to-LTL expansion at [backend/Allocation.v#L51](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/Allocation.v#L51); `transf_function_spec` already packages entrypoint and ABI-side facts at [backend/Allocproof.v#L1730](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/Allocproof.v#L1730); and the preservation proof already carries the move-heavy machinery, stackframe relation, and call/return structure at [backend/Allocproof.v#L1845](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/Allocproof.v#L1845) and [backend/Allocproof.v#L1895](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/Allocproof.v#L1895). So `LTLabi.v` makes sense only as a repackaging layer. If implemented literally, it risks duplicating the hardest part of `Allocproof`.

3. The boundary story needs to be stated against the exact LTL transformers, not a simplified "params white, rest undefined" intuition. At entry, `call_regs` preserves all registers and maps `Incoming` from caller `Outgoing` at [backend/LTL.v#L83](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/LTL.v#L83); only then does `undef_regs destroyed_at_function_entry` apply at [backend/LTL.v#L271](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/LTL.v#L271). Returns and externals similarly use whole-location-set transformers at [backend/LTL.v#L104](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/LTL.v#L104) and [backend/LTL.v#L116](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/LTL.v#L116). The plan gestures at this, but if the checker/spec is not written directly over those operators, it will be easy to prove the wrong invariant.

4. Phase 0 is stale. `transf_rtl_program_to_ltl'`, `transf_rtl_program_to_ltl`, and `transf_c_program_to_ltl` already exist at [driver/Compiler.v#L259](/home/alex/source/compcert/driver/Compiler.v#L259), [driver/Compiler.v#L272](/home/alex/source/compcert/driver/Compiler.v#L272), and [driver/Compiler.v#L312](/home/alex/source/compcert/driver/Compiler.v#L312), and the ordinary non-faulty theorem already exists at [driver/Complements.v#L280](/home/alex/source/compcert/driver/Complements.v#L280).

**Overall**
The plan basically makes sense. Targeting `LTL` is a good cut point: it is the first IR where post-regalloc `loc` structure is exposed, and overlap is already modeled by `Locmap.set` at [backend/Locations.v#L314](/home/alex/source/compcert/backend/Locations.v#L314). The staged `V1`/`V2` split also makes sense.

I do not see a fatal flaw in the overall theorem strategy. The main corrections I would make are: keep `V1` strictly aligned with the RTL fault model unless you intentionally want a stronger theorem, treat `LTLabi` as an extraction/repackaging of existing `Allocproof` facts rather than a fresh development, and rewrite Phase 0 to reflect what is already in the tree. If you make those adjustments, the plan is technically coherent.
